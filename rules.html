01The case

In 1983, a young Hollywood hopeful named Suzanne Macky is cast in the starring role of a new epic, Drowning in Space, shooting on the sound stages of E. F. Studios. Inexperienced actors, a terrible script and murder all vie to ruin the picture. Unfortunately for Miss Macky, she gets murdered. At the studio. We think.

Conflicting eyewitness reports point to a number of suspects who had access to the lot. A bungling crime lab can't pin down the time. Can the detectives of the Hollywood PD crack the case before the nation stops caring?

Each player is a detective. A promotion awaits whoever contributes most to solving the crime: developing hunches, issuing press releases and, of course, bringing the killer to justice.

The twist: in most mystery games you eliminate suspects. Here, every witness might be wrong. Evidence starts out barely better than a guess and gets more reliable every round. Wait for certainty and someone else gets the credit; act too early and you'll take an innocent person to trial.

02Quick start

Played before? Here's the whole game in six lines.

  • The app picks a secret killer from the 49 suspects. Every suspect has an outfit, a studio, a voice and an alibi time.
  • Rounds have 6 turns with 3 players, 4 with 4, and 3 with 5 or 6.
  • Each turn: call out Donut Shop trips, pick other destinations in secret, play action cards, then resolve locations 1 to 6.
  • Interrogations are right (round + 1) × 10% of the time: 20% in round 1, 50% by round 4.
  • After rounds 2 through 6, everyone may write a secret hunch.
  • The game ends when a guilty suspect is put on trial. Most points wins.

03Setup

You need one phone or tablet running the app (play.html), passed around the table. Everything secret happens behind a “pass the phone” screen, so nobody needs their own device.

  1. Choose a Lead Detective (suggestion: whoever most recently played a role in a trial). Enter detectives into the app in seating order, clockwise, starting with the Lead Detective.
  2. Choose how destinations are picked: on the phone (nothing else needed) or with dice (one die per player, faster at a big table).
  3. Optional extras from the printables: the suspect lineup poster for the middle of the table, a paper data sheet per player if you like pencil notes, and the location cards.
  4. Read the briefing aloud and start round 1.

The suspects

There are 7 outfits, 7 studios and 7 voices: 343 possible combinations, but only 49 of them are suspects. Each row of the lineup is one outfit, with the studio number increasing across the row. Every voice has its own color.

04A turn

  1. Donut calls. Starting with the Lead Detective and going clockwise, anyone may announce they're heading to the Donut Shop. Others can then join. Once everyone has had a chance, it's settled for the turn.
  2. Secret destinations. Everyone else picks a destination in secret: on the phone, or by setting a die and revealing all dice at once. You can't pick the Donut Shop in secret.
  3. Action cards. After destinations are revealed, anyone may play one card from their hand.
  4. Locations resolve in order, 1 through 6. The app walks the table through each one.

Whenever an order isn't otherwise specified, the Lead Detective goes first.

05The locations

Sharing a location. At the Diner, the Studio, Rodeo Drive and the crime lab, everyone at the location receives the same single piece of information. Crowding a location wastes turns. Going where nobody else goes is how you get ahead.

06Trials

  1. The prosecutor (or prosecutors, if several named the same suspect) states their evidence for each attribute out loud and names the time of the crime. Joint prosecutors may compare crime lab notes privately and must agree on a time.
  2. The app reveals the defendant's alibi time. The real crime time is never the alibi time, and is most often 30 minutes away from it.
  3. Every other detective secretly votes Guilty or Not guilty. Votes are revealed together.
  4. The prosecutor checks the suspect. If the defendant is guilty (not merely found guilty), the game ends. If innocent, they are cleared, points are scored, and the investigation continues.

If detectives at the Prosecutor's Office name different suspects, each gets a separate trial, in turn.

07End of a round

When the last turn of a round ends, the Lead Detective role passes one seat to the left, and witnesses become more reliable.

Hunches

After rounds 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6, each detective secretly either writes a hunch (the suspect they think did it) or passes. Say out loud whether you wrote one, but never who.

Hunch afterBase pointsIf you passed every time

A correct hunch scores its base points plus anything carried. A wrong hunch scores nothing and uses up the carry. Passing carries half the stake forward to your next hunch. Hunch correctly every time and you score 16 + 8 + 4 + 2 + 1 = 31.

When is it worth it? The payoffs tell you how sure you need to be. A press release pays off when you're more than 33% sure, a prosecution above about 43%, and a Guilty vote above 55%, a built-in presumption of innocence.

09Action cards

The action deck has 11 cards; the Box of Donuts deck has 4. Cards marked immediately resolve as soon as they're drawn. The others go to your hand to play on a later turn, after destinations are revealed (one card per detective per turn).

10Weighing the evidence

Later evidence is far more reliable, so it should count for more. The rule of thumb on the data sheet and in the app's notebook is almost exactly what a statistician would do:

Each mark is worth its round number. A report from round 3 counts 3. Total each attribute that way, then add a suspect's three totals together. The highest score is your most likely perpetrator.

Why it works: how much a matching report should shift your odds grows with the round, and in log-odds terms it grows almost exactly linearly. The true weight of a report, relative to a round-1 report:

Record second-hand information in the round it was gathered, not the round you heard it.

11How the odds work

Witness reliability by round

An interrogation gives the true attribute (round + 1) × 10% of the time. Otherwise it names one of the six wrong values at random. In round 1 a report is only a little better than a blind guess (14%). From round 9 on, every report is true.

The crime lab

The lab's accuracy never changes. Each reading is the true time plus a random error (two dice, minus seven, in five-minute steps): exactly right 17% of the time, within five minutes 44%, and never off by 30 minutes.

Alibis

The killer's crime time is their alibi time plus 5 to 55 minutes, most often 30. An innocent suspect's alibi is unrelated to the crime. At a trial, compare the alibi to your crime lab readings: an alibi about 30 minutes from the crime fits, while an alibi right at your estimated crime time points to innocence.

Why only 49 suspects?

Only one combination in seven is a real suspect. That works like an error-correcting code: a garbled report often points to a combination nobody has, and the real suspects stand out sooner. Even so, two attributes never pin down a single suspect, so all three locations matter.

12Rulings

TradingYou may offer first- or second-hand information and trade in pairs or as a group. You may not attach conditions (“don't pass this on”) or promise future information. Decide before the game whether bluffing is allowed; for a first game we suggest no lying, though you can always keep things to yourself.
Steal a PeekIn round 1 there is no “last round”, so it reveals information from the current round so far.
Strong-arm & SirensThese affect information-giving locations: the Diner, the Studio, Rodeo Drive and the crime lab.
Work During LunchJoining a location that already resolved this turn gives you the same information its visitors got. If nobody was there, you interview fresh.
Press releasesAt most one per category per game, three in total. Conflicting releases in the same category cancel each other.
Phone etiquetteWhen the phone says it's someone's private screen, everyone else looks away. The case log records every public action, so mistakes are easy to spot.
