Welcome to Interrogation!

A crime-solving game where confidence matters.

Interrogation is a game in the same vein as Clue, Sleuth, or Myster of the Abbey, but with a signficant twist: instead of eliminating suspecsts, a number of inaccurate eye-witness reports help you positively identify the culprit.

Quick Start

Read this section if you have played before and just need a refresh on the rules.
  • Each player needs a die, a pen/pencil, and a fact sheet.
  • Action cards go in two stacks in the middle, Box of Donuts cards and other action cards.
  • Players choose a destination, then the action for each location is executed.
  • 3, 4, 5, & 6 players have 6, 4, 3, & 3 turns per round, respectively.
  • The game ends when the perpetrator is tried.

Story

In the year 1983, a young Hollywood hopeful named Suzanne Macky has been cast in the staring role of a new epic titled Drowning in Space. The film is being shot on the sound stages of E. F. Studios. Unfortunately for the producers of the film, inexperienced actors, a terrible script, and murder all vie to ruin the movie. Unfortunately for Miss Macky, she gets murdered... at the studio... we think. Conflicting eye-witness reports point to a number of suspects who had access to the studio. A bungling crime lab cannot seem to pinpoint the time of the crime. Can the detectives of the Hollywood PD solve the crime before the nation stops caring about it?
Each player acts as a detective on the Hollywood police force trying to solve the mystery. A promotion is in store for the detective who contributes the most to solving the crime: developing hunches, releasing press reports, and of course bringing the perpetrator to justice.

Game Play

The game plays as a series of rounds plus a Trial Mode. All rounds have the same number of turns, dependant on the number of players (3, 4, 5, & 6 players have 6, 4, 3, & 3 turns per round, respectively). Each turn has two phases: in phase one, all players choose a destination for the turn; in phase two the actions for each destination are executed. During each turn players have the opportunity to collect information by interrogating people at a variety of locations. The information you gather is not perfect: you will need to interrogate the people at each location multiple times to get a clear picture of the correct information. As the game progresses, the likelihood of getting correct information goes up with each round. In addition to the three categories of attributes, each suspect also has an Alibi Time. The alibi time will never be the same as the Crime Time... it is most likely that the crime time and the alibi time are thirty minutes apart. Players can gather time information from the crime lab. The time information of the crime lab is different from the interrogations. During the entire game, the crime lab will give you a time that is more likely to be close to the crime time than to be far away. In fact the crime lab information will be on the button 16% of the time and no more than five minutes off 44% of the time. But it is possible to get a time twenty-five minutes off just 3% of the time. With such imprecise information it may appear to be quite difficult to solve this crime, but fortunately you can also rely on each other to share information. If you try to solve the crime on your own you will be bested by your fellow detectives. The game requires a javascript-enabled web browser, and is best played using a portable device such as a laptop or smart phone. At the start of the game, a Lead Detective must be chosen (suggestion: who most recently played a role in a trial?). Whenever an order is not otherwise specified, the Lead Detective goes first.

Selecting Destination

In phase one, players choose one of six locations.
1. The Diner
A hang-out for extras. The extras have seen some folks acting suspiciously and can describe their appearances.
2. E. F. Studios
The scene of the crime, consisting of 7 sound stages. Folks here heard what might have been a struggle and can identify the location.
3. Rodeo Drive
The movie stars here have heard some of the lowly extras say things that may be related to the crime. Not bothering to look at the person, they can only provide a description of the sound of the persons voice.
4. Police Station
Back at the police station you can get an estimate of the time of the crime from the crime lab... or you can collect an action card that may give you an advantage.
5. Donut Shop
Meet other detectives at the donut shop to trade info.
6. Prosecutors Office
Head to the prosecutors office when you are ready to make a press release or name the perpetrator.
First, anyone wishing to go to the donut shop can openly indicate his intentions. After a player says he is going to the donut shop, his destination is set for the round. Other players, going around clockwise can then say they will also go to the donut shop. Once each player has been given an opportunity to go to the donut shop, he may not change his mind during the turn.
All players not going to the donut shop then indicate their destinations secretly and simulatneously using a die. All players show the tops of the dice simultaneously. The number on top of the die indicates the players destination for the turn and cannot change unless an action card affecting location is played. An action card should be played after destination selection but before destination actions take place.

Location Actions

With all players destinations set, the location actions are executed in order from 1 to 6.
1. The Diner
If one player chose the diner, he presses the Diner button above to reveal a single piece of information about what the perpetrator was wearing. He then presses the Diner button again to clear the information. If more than one player chose the diner, then all players receive the same one piece of information.
2. E. F. Studios
If one player chose E. F. Studios, he presses the Studio button above to reveal a single piece of information about where the crime took place. He then presses the Studio button again to clear the information. If more than one player chose the studio, then all players receive the same one piece of information.
3. Rodeo Drive
If one player chose Rodeo Drive, he presses the Rodeo Drive button above to reveal a single piece of information about what the perpetrator sounds like. He then presses the Rodeo Drive button again to clear the information. If more than one player chose Rodeo Drive, then all players receive the same one piece of information.
4. Police Station
If one player chose the police station then he must choose between collecting information from the crime lab or taking an action card. If he chooses the crime lab, then he presses the Crime Lab button above to reveal a single piece of information about when the crime took place. He then presses the Crime Lab button again to clear the information. If he chooses to take an action card then he draws from the pile and silently reads it. Some action cards state that they are to be used immediately, while some can be held on to and played later at the end of phase one of a different turn. If more than one player chose the police station, then no choice between actions is available. The players all receive the same one piece of information from the crime lab.
5. Donut Shop
If only one player has chosen the donut shop, then he may take a Box of Donuts action card. If multiple players have chosen the donut shop, then they may negotiate between themselves what information they would like to share. The players may offer 1st hand or 2nd hand information and may negotiate to share information pair-wise or as a group. They may not put restrictions on information such as it may not be traded in the future, and they may not make promises of information in the future.
6. Prosecutors Office
If one player has chosen the prosecutors office, then he must choose between making a press release or naming the perpetrator. If the player chooses to make a press release, then he announces to all players any one attribute he believes the perpetrator has. He also writes the attribute in his case file for verification at the end of the game. If the player chooses to name the perpetrator, then the game enters the Trial Mode. At the end of the trial mode, if the defendant was guilty (as opposed to being found guilty), then the game ends. If the defendant was innocent (as opposed to being found not guilty), then the game continues. If multiple players chose the prosecutors office, then they must announce whether they wish to make a press release or name the perpetrator. Those players who wish to make a press release then must write down the attribute they wish to reveal and share it with other players wishing to make a press release. If they have listed the same attribute then they jointly make the press release. If they have listed different attributes in the same category, then no press release is allowed. If they have listed different attributes in different categories then they each make a separate press release. Once a press release for a given category has taken place, another press release for the same category is not permitted (a limit of three press releases per game). After all press releases have taken place, any players who earlier announced they would be naming the perpetrator must write down the attributes of the suspect they believe is the perpetrator and share it with the other detectives wishing to name a perpetrator. If they all agree on the identity of the perpetrator, then the trial goes forward with joint prosecution. If they disagree, then a separate trial mode for each named perpetrator will be held.

Trial Mode

When a player wishes to identify a suspect as the perpetrator, then he must first go to the prosecutors office. The suspect will be named as the defendant in a murder trial. The player (or players in the case of a joint prosecution) must then state the evidence against the defendant as well as state the time of the crime (in the case of a joint prosecution, the players may share their crime lab information privately and must come to a consensus on the time of the crime). After all evidence has been revealed, the player must then enter the suspects three attributes into the combo boxes above and press the Check Alibi button above. The time of the defendants alibi must then be announced. With these five pieces of information (the evidence for each of the three categories of attribute, the suspected time of the crime, and the time of the alibi) plus the evidence they have collected during the game, the other players must secretly cast a vote of Guilty or Not Guilty. Each player then reveals his vote. Finally, the prosecuting player must press the Check Suspect button above, which will reveal the guilt or innocence of the defendant. If the defendant is guilty, then the game is over and scores are tallied. If the defendant is innocent, scores are updated and the game continues.

End of a Round

After the set number of turns has been completed, the round ends. The current Lead Detective informs the players that the round is over and he presses the Next Round button above. If this is the end of the second, third, fourth, fifth, or sixth round, then players must choose to write down a hunch or pass on stating a hunch this round. Whether a hunch is being made should be announced to the other players, but the contents of the hunch should only be writen in private. The player to the left of the current lead detective becomes the new lead detective for the next round.

Scoring

Scoring in the game is based on how well you did in bringing the perpetrator to justice. There are four ways to win or lose points. The player with the most points wins. In the case of a tie, the player with the most overall evidence (1st hand & 2nd hand) wins.
Writing down hunches
Hunches are scored according to the round of the game during which the hunch was made. Hunches do not start until after the second round. A hunch is worth the points listed next to it plus any points carried over from a previous skipped hunch. When carrying forward hunch points, only one half of the points carry forward from round to round. There is no penalty to an incorrect hunch. If a player wrote a hunch each round and was correct in the second through sixth rounds, then he would be awarded 31 points. If a player did not make a hunch until the sixth round, carrying all points forward, he would be awarded 8 points.
Press Release
For each press release a player made, if the revealed attribute matches the perpetrator the player receives 10 points. Joint press releases are worth the same points. If a revealed attribute does not match the perpetrator, then the player loses 5 points.
Prosecution
A player who prosecutes a suspect who turns out to be guilty earns 16 points. A player who prosecutes a suspect who turns out to be innocent loses 12 points. In addition, the player gets +1 point for each Guilty vote. If there are multiple players prosecuting a suspect point values must be divided evenly, rounding away from zero. Each prosecuting player gets +1 point for each Guilty vote.
Voting
A player who votes Guilty in a trial where the defendant is guilty earns 6 points and loses 4 points if the defendant is innocent. A player who votes Not Guilty in a trial where the defendant is guilty loses 3 points and earns 7 points if the defendant is innocent.

Set Up

The game only requires simple items that should be available around the house, plus several print-outs and a way to run the javascript on this page.
  • 1 six-sided die per player
  • 1 pen/pencil per player
  • 1 data sheet print-out per player
  • 1 stack of action cards (print and cut up)
  • 1 stack of Box of Donuts action cards (print and cut up)
  • At least 1 print-out of the Fourty-nine Likely Suspects (share or give a copy to each player)
  • 1 Laptop/Desktop/Smart Phone (refered to as computer from here on)
Give each player a die, a pen/pencil, and a data sheet. Place the two stacks of action cards in the middle, face down. Let each player see the Likely Suspects print out, then place it in the middle of the table. Read the Story and Game Play sections above. Decide upon a Lead Detective and start playing. Once the game begins it is of CRITICAL importance to the game that this webpage not be reloaded or navigated away from. Doing so would be the equivalent of changing the suspect in the middle of a game of Clue. Each time the webpage is loaded, a javascript action takes place to secretly decide upon the perpetrator from the list of likely suspects. If the combo boxes above do not start out blank, or pressing the various buttons does not alter the state of the combo boxes, try enabling javascript in your browser. Whatever the type of computer used, players will need to easily access it privately throughout the game. A smart phone is the most suitable device.

Cheating

This page makes no attempts to prevent a player from clicking the wrong item or clicking an item more than once. To help prevent cheating, the page always displays two pieces of information about the game: the number of pieces of information revealed, and the last button pressed. As long as each player knows the expected number of clicks and last button pressed, cheating can be identified.

Probability

This section is intended for anyone with an interest in probabilities, and the role they play in this game. There are 49 likely suspects out of a possible 343 combinations of attributes. Reducing the number of possible suspects helps the player in two ways: first, the player only has to identify the perpetrator from a group of 49 suspects and second, whenever the player evaluates a combination of attributes there is only a one in seven chance that the combination maps to a suspect. When inaccurate data is in play, this can greatly improve the odds of choosing the perpetrator correctly. The basis of the game is posterior probability... the likelyhood of suspect A being guilty given that I received a piece of evidence implicating him. As the game progresses, the quality of information improves. In round one, you are only marginally more likely to get the attribute of the perpetrator than you are to get any other random attribute. By round 4, each interrogation gives you the correct information half the time. Since the picture you get is always incomplete, the more information you can aquire (through interrogations and information sharing), the better. To play well you must balance the quantity and quality of information you have against the information other players have. Waiting until you are sure you have the correct suspect will almost certainly cost you the game.
